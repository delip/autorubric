### CANNOT_ASSESS Handling
Strategies (applied by the single `score_reports` core, so binary CANNOT_ASSESS and multi-choice NA are handled identically wherever a score is computed): `SKIP` (excludes the criterion from both numerator and denominator), `ZERO`, `PARTIAL` (configurable), `FAIL` (weight-sign-aware worst case). Mechanics + `FAIL` detail: see [Score Calculation](grading-flow.md).

Judge-call failure routing (`infrastructure`/`parse` → CANNOT_ASSESS / `na=True`, excluded under SKIP; `unknown` → conservative worst case; forced-choice no-NA infra/parse abstain → `na=True` with `selected_index=None`/`selected_label=None`) is detailed in [Grading Flow](grading-flow.md).

### Decision-model abstention
A decision-model judge (`DecisionModelConfig`) abstains only where its question **offers** an abstain outcome, so abstention is a property of the framing (question and answer rules: [Grading Flow](grading-flow.md)):

- **Binary `binary_framing="choice"`** offers `CANNOT_ASSESS` (with the LLM judge's `CANNOT_ASSESS_DEFINITION`); selecting it gives `verdict=CANNOT_ASSESS`, the same epistemic abstention an LLM judge returns. It is the only binary framing that can abstain: the Noul framings (`"noul"`, `"noul_framed"`, the default) have only yes/no and always issue MET or UNMET; a probability exactly at `decision_threshold` resolves to the weight-sign worst case, never to an abstention.
- **Multi-choice `Choice`** (every nominal criterion; ordinal under the default `ordinal_framing="choice"`) offers every option of the effective rubric, so the NA option — authored, or the auto-injected `CANONICAL_NA_OPTION` under `auto_na_option=True` — is a choice; selecting it gives `na=True` at that option's index, the structural analog of CANNOT_ASSESS. With `auto_na_option=False` and no authored NA option there is nothing to abstain with.
- **Multi-choice `Score`** (`ordinal_framing="score"`) never offers the NA option — its levels are the non-NA options — so a Score-framed criterion **cannot abstain**; the NA option is also absent from its `probabilities`.
- **Failures are not verdicts.** A criterion whose question cannot be built, and an answer that is missing, of the wrong type or malformed, raise `ValueError`, which `classify_grading_error` routes as `parse`, the abstaining category of the judge-call failure routing above, for that criterion only. A failed request fails every criterion posed in it with the request's category — `infrastructure`/`parse` abstain, `unknown` takes the worst case — exactly as an LLM judge's failed call would (failure scope: [Grading Flow](grading-flow.md)).

Both abstentions then flow through `score_reports` under the configured `CannotAssessStrategy` exactly like an LLM judge's.

**In a cascade, abstentions and errors escalate.** With `CriterionGrader(escalation=EscalationConfig(...))`, a decision-model vote that abstained (`CANNOT_ASSESS` or an NA option) or errored (any category) is always escalated, whatever its `confidence` — "I can't tell" is what the fallback is for, and a failed judgment has no confidence — so a Score-framed criterion, which cannot abstain, escalates only on an error or low confidence. The escalation judges' own failures route as above (`infrastructure`/`parse` abstain, `unknown` → worst case). When every escalation vote abstains — the judges answered `CANNOT_ASSESS` / the NA option, or their calls failed as `infrastructure`/`parse` — the criterion abstains (and counts under the `CannotAssessStrategy`), never falling back to the decision model's superseded vote. Its `error` comes from `_aggregate_error` over the escalation votes: set only when every one of them failed, `None` (with `is_error` false) when the judges genuinely abstained ([Grading Flow](grading-flow.md)).

### NA Handling (multi-choice metrics)
NA is the multi-choice *structural* analog of binary CANNOT_ASSESS, though a **distinct kind of abstention** (see *CANNOT_ASSESS Handling (binary metrics)*). At metrics time, `compute_metrics(..., na_mode=NAMode)` mirrors `cannot_assess: CannotAssessMode` one-for-one:

- `"exclude"` — drop pairs where either side is NA (default).
- `"as_unmet"` — remap NA → the score-minimizing non-NA option via the shared `Criterion.worst_scored_option()` (weight-sign aware, same helper as the `unknown`-error path).
- `"as_category"` — keep NA as a distinct categorical column. **Refused for ordinal criteria with an NA option** (raises `ValueError`): NA has no ordinal position, so quadratic-weighted kappa would assign it a meaningless index-based distance.

`compute_metrics` builds criteria from author-space `dataset.rubric`, but a prediction may reference an **auto-injected** NA option at index `N = len(author.options)` (out of author range) **or be a genuine no-option abstain (`selected_index=None`)**. Per multi-choice criterion, metrics reconstructs the effective criterion via `Criterion.with_guaranteed_na_option()` **iff an out-of-range OR `None` prediction is observed**, then normalizes any `None` prediction to that effective NA index — so injected-NA predictions and forced-choice error-abstains count as NA (FP/FN, `na_kappa`, filtering) under every `na_mode`, never as option 0, while forced-choice runs without abstains gain no spurious NA column. A per-judge cell where the judge cast **no vote** (a cascade escalation judge on a criterion that was not escalated, a judge absent from an item) is masked as missing, not read as a `None` prediction: it never triggers the reconstruction and is neither a prediction nor an abstention (see [Metrics](metrics.md)). Consequence: once an ordinal criterion gains an NA option (authored, auto-injected + predicted, or induced by a `None` abstain), `na_mode="as_category"` is refused for it.

The old `"as_worst"` literal was renamed to `"as_category"` (it kept NA as a column, did not remap it); the old value raises a clear `ValueError`. The NAStats counts (`na_count_true`, `na_count_pred`, `na_false_positive`, `na_false_negative`, `na_kappa`) are mode-independent: FP/FN counters increment before any `exclude` skip, and `na_kappa` is computed from unfiltered per-criterion pred/true.

### CANNOT_ASSESS Handling (binary metrics) and the two kinds of abstention
Binary criteria get a `MetricsResult.cannot_assess_stats: CannotAssessStats | None` block, the structural parallel of multi-choice `na_stats: NAStats`. Populated whenever the rubric has ≥1 binary criterion (mirroring `na_stats` for ≥1 multi-choice criterion); reports the dichotomized {CANNOT_ASSESS, not-CANNOT_ASSESS} agreement (`ca_count_true`, `ca_count_pred`, `ca_false_positive`, `ca_false_negative`, `ca_kappa` — `None` when undefined). Like NAStats these counts are **mode-independent** (from raw per-criterion verdicts before any `cannot_assess` filtering) and surfaced in `summary()` only (NOT `to_dataframe()`).

**Two kinds of abstention (why two types, not one).** CANNOT_ASSESS and NA both flow through the same SKIP path and both get a parallel dichotomized-kappa stats block, but are semantically **distinct**:

- Binary **CANNOT_ASSESS** = the judge cannot determine MET-vs-UNMET — an *epistemic* abstention on a yes/no question.
- Multi-choice **NA** = "not applicable / cannot pick an applicable option" — abstaining because no scored category fits, a statement about the *option space*.

Tracked by **separate** types (`CannotAssessStats`, `ca_`-prefixed, vs. `NAStats`, `na_`-prefixed) to keep the distinction explicit while preserving the structural analogy.

### Coverage diagnostics (how much of the raw sample survived exclusion)
Under the **`exclude` handling mode only** (both `cannot_assess="exclude"` and `na_mode="exclude"`), `compute_metrics` builds per-criterion and aggregate `CoverageStats` via `_build_coverage_stats`, recording how much of the raw paired sample survived the union of all exclusion reasons. Under `as_unmet`/`as_category` nothing is union-excluded, so coverage would be trivially `1.0` and the stats are left `None`. The **raw denominator** counts every ground-truth-bearing item, including those lost to a grading error: an `n_errored_items` counter increments at the `item_result.error is not None` skip site (only an item whose grading raised; see *Items no judge judged* below), so `error_rate`, `judge_abstain_rate`, and `gt_abstain_rate` all share one consistent denominator (`items_with_ground_truth + n_errored_items`). `n_covered` equals the per-criterion `n_samples` (post-exclusion); `union_exclusion_rate = 1 - coverage`; every rate honours undefined→None at a zero denominator while counts stay `int`. The aggregate rollup pools across criteria (raw denominator × n_criteria; covered = sum of per-criterion `n_samples`). A warning is appended when any errored item was excluded, and the top-level `n_samples` is the sum of per-criterion `n_samples`.

### Items no judge judged
When every judge's judgment of every criterion of an item failed — every criterion report carries an `error`; an `EnsembleCriterionReport` carries one only when all its votes errored — the grader still returns an ordinary report: infrastructure/parse failures abstain, so under the default `SKIP` nothing is left to score and `score_reports` returns its empty-denominator `0.0`, while `unknown` failures take the worst case. That report is unchanged, for either judge kind, so an LLM grader whose every call fails keeps the report it always produced (existing tests pin its `score == 0.0`), and a decision model's failed request produces the identical report (a decision model makes one request per item, so one failed request is enough to produce it). The metrics layer treats that report exactly as it always has: it is **not** an errored item — only `ItemResult.error` (grading raised) makes one — so it counts in `n_items`, not in `n_errored_items` / `CoverageStats.n_errored` or the "excluded from metrics because grading errored" warning; its error-abstentions are handled by the configured `cannot_assess`/`na_mode` like any abstention (excluded under `"exclude"`, counted as UNMET and in `cannot_assess_stats` under `"as_unmet"`, exactly as on an item with only some criteria errored); its `0.0` is paired with the item's true score in `score_rmse`/`score_mae`/the correlations/`bias`; and the per-item pooled path (`_compute_per_item_pooled_metrics`) scores it the same way. Per judge likewise: a judge whose every vote on an item failed keeps its report's `judge_scores` entry for that item ([Metrics](metrics.md)). So `compute_metrics` still returns a result when every item's every judgment failed. Identical reports thus give identical metrics whatever the judge kind (pinned for LLM graders whose calls fail and against an all-LLM mirror of a failed decision-model request, `tests/metrics/test_all_errored_items.py`, `tests/decision/test_decision_eval.py`). A caller who wants such items out of its metrics can recognize them structurally — every criterion report's `is_error` — and drop them before calling `compute_metrics`.
