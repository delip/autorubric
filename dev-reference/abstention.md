### CANNOT_ASSESS Handling
Strategies (applied by the single `score_reports` core, so binary CANNOT_ASSESS and multi-choice NA are handled identically wherever a score is computed): `SKIP` (excludes the criterion from both numerator and denominator), `ZERO`, `PARTIAL` (configurable), `FAIL` (weight-sign-aware worst case). Mechanics + `FAIL` detail: see [Score Calculation](grading-flow.md). Under `SKIP` an item every criterion of which abstains has nothing left to score and so no score (*Items with nothing left to score* below); `ZERO`/`PARTIAL`/`FAIL` keep abstentions in the score, so abstentions never leave them without one.

Judge-call failure routing (`infrastructure`/`parse` → CANNOT_ASSESS / `na=True`, excluded under SKIP; `unknown` → conservative worst case; forced-choice no-NA infra/parse abstain → `na=True` with `selected_index=None`/`selected_label=None`) is detailed in [Grading Flow](grading-flow.md). A stand-in verdict keeps its criterion routed, but an item whose every criterion's judgment failed has no score at all (*Items whose every criterion's judgment failed* below).

### Decision-model abstention
A decision-model judge (`DecisionModelConfig`) abstains only where its question **offers** an abstain outcome, so abstention is a property of the framing (question and answer rules: [Grading Flow](grading-flow.md)):

- **Binary `binary_framing="choice"`** offers `CANNOT_ASSESS` (with the LLM judge's `CANNOT_ASSESS_DEFINITION`); selecting it gives `verdict=CANNOT_ASSESS`, the same epistemic abstention an LLM judge returns. It is the only binary framing that can abstain: the Noul framings (`"noul"`, `"noul_framed"`, the default) have only yes/no and always issue MET or UNMET; a probability exactly at `decision_threshold` resolves to the weight-sign worst case, never to an abstention.
- **Multi-choice `Choice`** (every nominal criterion; ordinal under the default `ordinal_framing="choice"`) offers every option of the effective rubric, so the NA option — authored, or the auto-injected `CANONICAL_NA_OPTION` under `auto_na_option=True` — is a choice; selecting it gives `na=True` at that option's index, the structural analog of CANNOT_ASSESS. With `auto_na_option=False` and no authored NA option there is nothing to abstain with.
- **Multi-choice `Score`** (`ordinal_framing="score"`) never offers the NA option — its levels are the non-NA options — so a Score-framed criterion **cannot abstain**; the NA option is also absent from its `probabilities`.
- **Failures are not verdicts.** A criterion whose question cannot be built, and an answer that is missing, of the wrong type or malformed, raise `ValueError`, which `classify_grading_error` routes as `parse`, the abstaining category of the judge-call failure routing above, for that criterion only. A failed request fails every criterion posed in it with the request's category — `infrastructure`/`parse` abstain, `unknown` takes the worst case — exactly as an LLM judge's failed call would (failure scope: [Grading Flow](grading-flow.md)).

Both abstentions then flow through `score_reports` under the configured `CannotAssessStrategy` exactly like an LLM judge's.

**In a cascade, abstentions and errors escalate.** With `CriterionGrader(escalation=EscalationConfig(...))`, a decision-model vote that abstained (`CANNOT_ASSESS` or an NA option) or errored (any category) is always escalated, whatever its `confidence` — "I can't tell" is what the fallback is for, and a failed judgment has no confidence — so a Score-framed criterion, which cannot abstain, escalates only on an error or low confidence. The escalation judges' own failures route as above (`infrastructure`/`parse` abstain, `unknown` → worst case). When every escalation vote abstains — the judges answered `CANNOT_ASSESS` / the NA option, or their calls failed as `infrastructure`/`parse` — the criterion abstains (and counts under the `CannotAssessStrategy`), never falling back to the decision model's superseded vote. Its `error` comes from `_aggregate_error` over the escalation votes: set only when every one of them failed, `None` (with `is_error` false) when the judges genuinely abstained ([Grading Flow](grading-flow.md)). When every criterion of an item escalated and every escalation vote on each failed, every criterion's judgment failed and the item has no score (*Items whose every criterion's judgment failed* below), whether the decision model's request failed (which escalates every criterion) or it judged every criterion with too little confidence; in the latter case the decision model keeps its `judge_scores` entry. When the escalation judges answered what a failed request escalated, the item is judged and scored from their verdicts, while the decision model, which judged nothing, has a `None` `judge_scores` entry. An offline replay (`replay_escalation`) escalates them by the same rule, and `escalation_stats` counts such a pair in `escalation_rate` but in no accuracy (neither decision-model accuracy, nor the fallback's, which is measured only where the decision model made a prediction to compare it with), since an abstention or a failed judgment is no prediction; so is a final verdict that stands in for the escalation judges' failed calls, so a deferred pair whose escalation votes all failed is in neither escalated accuracy ([Offline Cascade Replay, Diagnostics and Calibration](grading-flow.md)).

### NA Handling (multi-choice metrics)
NA is the multi-choice *structural* analog of binary CANNOT_ASSESS, though a **distinct kind of abstention** (see *CANNOT_ASSESS Handling (binary metrics)*). At metrics time, `compute_metrics(..., na_mode=NAMode)` mirrors `cannot_assess: CannotAssessMode` one-for-one:

- `"exclude"` — drop pairs where either side is NA (default).
- `"as_unmet"` — remap NA → the score-minimizing non-NA option via the shared `Criterion.worst_scored_option()` (weight-sign aware, same helper as the `unknown`-error path).
- `"as_category"` — keep NA as a distinct categorical column. **Refused for ordinal criteria with an NA option** (raises `ValueError`): NA has no ordinal position, so quadratic-weighted kappa would assign it a meaningless index-based distance.

`compute_metrics` builds criteria from author-space `dataset.rubric`, but a prediction may reference an **auto-injected** NA option at index `N = len(author.options)` (out of author range) **or be a genuine no-option abstain (`selected_index=None`)**. Per multi-choice criterion, metrics reconstructs the effective criterion via `Criterion.with_guaranteed_na_option()` **iff an out-of-range OR `None` prediction is observed**, then normalizes any `None` prediction to that effective NA index — so injected-NA predictions and forced-choice error-abstains count as NA (FP/FN, `na_kappa`, filtering) under every `na_mode`, never as option 0, while forced-choice runs without abstains gain no spurious NA column. A per-judge cell where the judge cast **no vote** (a cascade escalation judge on a criterion that was not escalated, a judge absent from an item) is masked as missing, not read as a `None` prediction: it never triggers the reconstruction and is neither a prediction nor an abstention (see [Metrics](metrics.md)). Nor does a failed cell (*Items with only some criteria failed* below) or a judge's failed vote: only a judgment some metric reads decides whether a criterion needs its NA option, so a failed forced-choice stand-in (`selected_index=None`) adds no NA column and never makes `na_mode="as_category"` refuse an ordinal criterion. Consequence: once an ordinal criterion gains an NA option (authored, auto-injected + predicted, or induced by a `None` abstain), `na_mode="as_category"` is refused for it.

The old `"as_worst"` literal was renamed to `"as_category"` (it kept NA as a column, did not remap it); the old value raises a clear `ValueError`. The NAStats counts (`na_count_true`, `na_count_pred`, `na_false_positive`, `na_false_negative`, `na_kappa`) are mode-independent: FP/FN counters increment before any `exclude` skip, and `na_kappa` is computed from unfiltered per-criterion pred/true.

### CANNOT_ASSESS Handling (binary metrics) and the two kinds of abstention
Binary criteria get a `MetricsResult.cannot_assess_stats: CannotAssessStats | None` block, the structural parallel of multi-choice `na_stats: NAStats`. Populated whenever the rubric has ≥1 binary criterion (mirroring `na_stats` for ≥1 multi-choice criterion); reports the dichotomized {CANNOT_ASSESS, not-CANNOT_ASSESS} agreement (`ca_count_true`, `ca_count_pred`, `ca_false_positive`, `ca_false_negative`, `ca_kappa` — `None` when undefined). Like NAStats these counts are **mode-independent** (from raw per-criterion verdicts before any `cannot_assess` filtering) and surfaced in `summary()` only (NOT `to_dataframe()`).

**Two kinds of abstention (why two types, not one).** CANNOT_ASSESS and NA both flow through the same SKIP path and both get a parallel dichotomized-kappa stats block, but are semantically **distinct**:

- Binary **CANNOT_ASSESS** = the judge cannot determine MET-vs-UNMET — an *epistemic* abstention on a yes/no question.
- Multi-choice **NA** = "not applicable / cannot pick an applicable option" — abstaining because no scored category fits, a statement about the *option space*.

Tracked by **separate** types (`CannotAssessStats`, `ca_`-prefixed, vs. `NAStats`, `na_`-prefixed) to keep the distinction explicit while preserving the structural analogy.

### Coverage diagnostics (how much of the raw sample survived exclusion)
Under the **`exclude` handling mode only** (both `cannot_assess="exclude"` and `na_mode="exclude"`), `compute_metrics` builds per-criterion and aggregate `CoverageStats` via `_build_coverage_stats`, recording how much of the raw paired sample survived the union of all exclusion reasons. Under `as_unmet`/`as_category` nothing is union-excluded, so coverage would be trivially `1.0` and the stats are left `None`. The **raw denominator** counts every ground-truth-bearing item, including those lost to a grading error: an `n_errored_items` counter increments at the `_is_errored_item(item_result)` skip site (an item whose grading raised, whose report is an error report, or every criterion report of which `is_error`; see *Items whose every criterion's judgment failed* below and [Metrics](metrics.md)), so `error_rate`, `judge_abstain_rate`, and `gt_abstain_rate` all share one consistent denominator (`items_with_ground_truth + n_errored_items`). `n_covered` equals the per-criterion `n_samples` (post-exclusion); `n_errored` counts the errored pairs: per criterion, `n_errored_items` plus that criterion's failed cells (*Items with only some criteria failed* below), with `error_rate = n_errored / n_total`; the abstain counts read the judged cells only, so a failed cell counts as errored, never as an abstention on either side; `union_exclusion_rate = 1 - coverage`; every rate honours undefined→None at a zero denominator while counts stay `int`. The aggregate rollup pools across criteria through `_build_coverage_stats` too (raw denominator × n_criteria; covered, abstained and errored = the sums of the per-criterion counts), so its `n_errored` is a pair count like its `n_total` (an errored item counts once per criterion) and its `error_rate` is unchanged for a run whose only errors are errored items. A warning is appended when any errored item was excluded (in every handling mode, `CoverageStats` or not), a second (`_failed_judgments_warning`: `"<m> criterion judgment(s) on graded items failed and were left out of the criterion-level metrics."`) when any failed cell was, and the top-level `n_samples` is the sum of per-criterion `n_samples`.

### Items whose every criterion's judgment failed
When every criterion's judgment of an item failed — every criterion report carries an `error` (`is_error`); an `EnsembleCriterionReport` carries one only when all its aggregated (non-`superseded`) votes errored — the item has **no score**, under every `CannotAssessStrategy` (#18). Each criterion report keeps its failure stand-in (`CANNOT_ASSESS` / the NA option for `infrastructure`/`parse`, the weight-sign worst case for `unknown`) and its own category-prefixed `error`, but the item report, assembled by `_ensemble_evaluation_report` (the check is `_every_judgment_failed` in `scoring.py`), has `score`, `raw_score` and `llm_raw_score` `None` and `error` = `"Every criterion's judgment failed: ..."` (format and mechanics: [Score Calculation](grading-flow.md)). The report is the same for either judge kind: an LLM grader whose every call failed, a decision model whose one request failed (one request per item, so one failure is enough), a panel every judge of which failed on every criterion, and a cascade every criterion of which escalated and got only failed escalation votes, even where the decision model judged it. A panel criterion with one genuine vote is judged, and so is every criterion of a cascade whose escalation judges answered what a failed decision-model request escalated; such an item is scored as usual, with `error` `None`. Stand-ins still count where some criterion was judged: an `unknown` failure's worst case counts against the score of an item with other judged criteria, and `infrastructure`/`parse` abstentions are left out under `SKIP`, as before (`test_a_failed_criterion_among_judged_ones_is_scored_by_its_stand_in`). Per judge, `CriterionGrader.aggregate` gives a judge every vote of which on the item failed a `None` `judge_scores` entry, whatever the category or strategy; a cascade's decision model that judged keeps its score, even on an item that fails. Pinned by `test_an_item_whose_every_judgment_failed_has_no_score`, `test_the_error_of_an_item_nothing_judged_names_the_first_failure` and `test_a_judge_whose_every_vote_failed_has_no_score_for_the_item` (`tests/graders/test_error_routing.py`).

Downstream the item is a failed grade like any other: `EvalRunner` records its report's `error` as `ItemResult.error`, so it is a failed item (what that means for the run, its cost totals and a resumed run: [Key Conventions](conventions.md)). `compute_metrics` treats it as an **errored item** (`_is_errored_item`: `ItemResult.error` set, the report's `error` set, or every criterion report `is_error`): left out of every metric, its verdicts included, counted in `n_errored_items` (each of its pairs in `CoverageStats.n_errored` on the per-criterion path, under the `exclude` modes) and named in the "excluded from metrics because grading errored" warning, on the per-item pooled path (`_compute_per_item_pooled_metrics`) too. When every item with ground truth is errored, either path raises `ValueError("No valid items with ground truth found")` instead of returning metrics built on fabricated scores ([Metrics](metrics.md)). The structural check (every criterion report `is_error`) recognizes such an item in a run saved before these reports carried an `error` — it stored a fabricated score (e.g. `0.0` under `SKIP`, the worst case for `unknown` failures) and no error at either level — so metrics recomputed from old runs leave it out too; per judge, `_judge_failed_every_vote` drops a saved number the same way. The loaded `EvalResult` itself still shows such an item as it was saved: `ItemResult.error` `None`, so not in `failed_items`, and its fabricated score in `get_scores()`. Identical reports give identical metrics whatever the judge kind: pinned for LLM graders whose calls fail, for reports in both the current and the saved-before style (`STYLES`), and against an all-LLM mirror of a failed decision-model request (`tests/metrics/test_all_errored_items.py`; `test_a_failed_request_fails_like_an_llm_grader_whose_calls_all_failed` in `tests/decision/test_decision_eval.py`).

### Items with only some criteria failed
An item with only some criteria failed is no errored item (`test_an_item_with_some_criteria_errored_is_still_scored`): its judged criteria count as usual, and it keeps its score (built from its judged criteria and the stand-ins, above), so it has a score pair. Each criterion whose judgment failed on it — `_judgment_failed(criterion_report)`: its `is_error` (for an `EnsembleCriterionReport`, every aggregated vote failed), or a legacy dict-shaped report's `"error"` — is a **failed cell**: its stand-in verdict (the abstention of an `infrastructure`/`parse` failure, the worst case of an `unknown` one, or a forced-choice `selected_index=None`) is no judgment, so it is neither a prediction nor an abstention, in every `cannot_assess` and `na_mode`, for binary and multi-choice criteria alike. `compute_metrics` keeps a `per_criterion_failed` mask item-aligned with the per-criterion prediction and truth lists, and every criterion-level consumer reads the judged cells only (`_judged_cells`): a failed cell is left out of the per-criterion metrics and the aggregate scalars, of `NAStats`/`CannotAssessStats` (its ground-truth side with it), of the bootstrap (each replicate drops its resampled failed cells) and of the pooled path (neither a point nor in `n_abstain`; a scale whose only cells failed gets no `pooled_by_scale` entry); it never triggers the NA-option reconstruction (above); it counts as an errored pair in `CoverageStats.n_errored` (*Coverage diagnostics* above); and `_failed_judgments_warning` reports how many were left out. Per-judge metrics skip a judge's failed votes the same way, so the aggregate of a one-judge ensemble equals its per-judge metrics by construction. Pinned by `tests/metrics/test_failed_criteria.py` (every handling mode and criterion kind, coverage, the NA and CANNOT_ASSESS stats, the bootstrap, the pooled path, and a real `CriterionGrader` run in which one call fails per failure category).

**A failure stand-in is never ground truth.** The verdicts above stand in for failed judge calls so that every criterion stays routed; they are not judgments, so they never give an item a score on their own (above), and `fill_ground_truth` never saves one as a label. `_extract_ground_truth_from_report` (`utils.py`) raises `ValueError` for any criterion report whose `is_error` is set, before reading its verdict, and `fill_ground_truth` leaves the item out as it leaves out any item it fails to label (issue #22). One failed criterion is enough, since a label covers every criterion. A panel or cascade criterion with at least one genuine aggregated vote has no `error` and is labelled from those votes (a cascade whose decision-model request failed is labelled from its escalation judges). Its guard for a multi-choice abstain that selected no option stays for reports without an `error`. Pinned by `tests/utils/test_fill_ground_truth.py` (each failure category, one failed criterion, a panel with one genuine vote), `TestWholeRequestFailure` in `tests/decision/test_decision_grader.py` and `TestEscalationJudgeFailures` in `tests/decision/test_decision_cascade.py`.

### Items with nothing left to score
When every criterion's final verdict abstains (`CANNOT_ASSESS` or an NA option) but not every criterion's judgment failed — the judges answered, or some of the abstentions stand in for `infrastructure`/`parse` failures — `SKIP` leaves no criterion to score and `score_reports` returns `None`, normalized or raw: the report has `score`, `raw_score` and `llm_raw_score` `None` and `error` `None` (#18). It is neither a failed grade nor an errored item: `EvalRunner` records no `ItemResult.error` (only `get_scores()`, which skips every score-less report, leaves it out), and `compute_metrics` counts its verdicts in the criterion-level metrics, `cannot_assess_stats`/`na_stats`, the per-judge metrics and inter-judge agreement, but gives it no score pair. Ground truth whose labels all abstain has no true score (`Rubric.compute_score` / `RubricDataset.compute_weighted_score` return `None`), which likewise leaves its item out of the score pairs only; when no graded item has a score pair, `compute_metrics` refuses with a `ValueError` that says so ([Errored items and score pairs](metrics.md)). A judge none of whose verdicts is left to score has a `None` `judge_scores` entry. `ZERO`/`PARTIAL`/`FAIL` keep every abstention in the score, so under them an item with any judged criterion always has a score; zero-weight criteria are scored under every strategy (a report whose only scored criteria weigh zero scores `0.0`). Pinned by `test_an_item_with_nothing_left_to_score_has_no_score` (`tests/graders/test_error_routing.py`), `test_skip_strategy_all_cannot_assess_has_no_score` (`tests/graders/test_cannot_assess.py`), `test_an_item_with_nothing_left_to_score_counts_only_its_verdicts`, `test_an_item_with_nothing_left_to_score_counts_for_each_judge`, `test_an_item_whose_ground_truth_leaves_nothing_to_score_leaves_the_score_pairs` and `test_no_score_pairs_at_all_is_refused_with_its_cause` (`tests/metrics/test_all_errored_items.py`), `test_ground_truth_with_nothing_left_to_score_has_no_score` (`tests/graders/test_score_unification.py`) and `tests/test_scoring.py`.
