Feedback for rubric improvement

unidimensional Clarity & Precision
default: The rubric criteria are multi-barreled, conflating several distinct attributes within single scoring dimensions. For example, Criterion 1 combines 'atomicity' (single assertion) with 'linguistic precision' (avoiding vague terms), while Criterion 4 conflates 'scientific status' (not settled/impossible) with 'reasoning type' (cross-domain or forward-in-time projection). [Affects: #1, #4]
deterministic_assessability Reliability Predictors
default: The rubric includes criteria that require subjective judgment and external knowledge not present in the submission text. Specifically, Criterion 1 relies on a subjective prediction of how 'different SMEs would interpret' the claim, and Criterion 3 requires external knowledge to determine if a claim 'depends on inaccessible proprietary information.' [Affects: #1, #3, #4]
double_barreled Anti-Patterns (Standalone)
default: The submission contains criteria that bundle multiple distinct requirements into a single assessment. For example, Criterion 4 requires a claim to be nontrivial, plausible, not settled by consensus, not impossible, not already achieved, and require specific types of reasoning and projection. [Affects: #1, #2, #4]
overly_verbose Anti-Patterns (Standalone)
default: Criteria 3 and 4 contain redundant phrasing and exhaustive lists that could be significantly condensed without losing meaning. For instance, criterion 4 uses multiple clauses to describe a 'nontrivial' claim that are largely synonymous. [Affects: #3, #4]
unanchored_subjectivity Anti-Patterns (Standalone)
default: The rubric relies on subjective and comparative terms such as 'enough technical detail' (Criterion 2), 'nontrivial reasoning' (Criterion 4), and 'substantive ambiguity' (Criterion 1) without providing objective thresholds or reference points, forcing raters to apply personal standards. [Affects: #1, #2, #3, #4]
no_negative_criteria LLM-Judge Anti-Patterns
default: The rubric consists of four criteria (clear, specified, automatable, and projective), all of which are assigned a positive weight of 5.0. There are no negative-weight or penalty criteria included in the structure.
unanchored_grounding LLM-Judge Anti-Patterns
default: The rubric requires evaluators to verify claims against 'existing scientific consensus' (Criterion 4) and the capabilities of an 'automated AI feasibility system' (Criterion 3) without providing any external references, gold standards, or bounded audit procedures to define these benchmarks. This forces the evaluator to rely on an undefined external body of knowledge (scientific consensus) and an undefined technical standard (AI system capabilities), fitting manifestations (B) and (C) of the criterion. [Affects: #3, #4]
